rapportd lets your **Apple devices nearby find and talk to each other**, for Handoff, Universal Clipboard and the other Continuity features. It's part of macOS and safe.

## Key points

- rapportd connects your Mac with your iPhone, iPad and other Macs nearby.
- It's rarely busy.
- Quitting it doesn't help for long: macOS starts it again.

## What rapportd does

When you copy on your iPhone and paste on your Mac, or pick up a web page from one device on another, your devices first had to find each other on the network and confirm they belong to you. rapportd handles that conversation.

## "Do you want rapportd to accept incoming network connections?"

If your Mac's firewall is on, it may ask this. It's genuine: rapportd needs incoming connections so your other devices can reach your Mac. **Allow** it if you use Handoff, Universal Clipboard or other Continuity features; **Deny** if you don't, and those features won't reach this Mac.

## Why is rapportd using CPU?

It's rarely busy. Brief activity is normal when devices come near, wake up or join the same network.

In one reading of a MacBook Pro (M1 Max, 64 GB) on October 10, 2026, rapportd was using **0.6% of the CPU** and **15.0 MB of memory**.

## Can I quit or remove rapportd?

Stopping it doesn't help for long: macOS starts it again when it's needed. It's part of macOS, so it can't be removed.

## How to check it

1. Open **Activity Monitor**: press Command-Space, type "Activity Monitor" and press Return.
2. Type **rapportd** in the search field at the top right.
3. Look at **% CPU** and **Memory**.

## Questions

### Is rapportd a virus?

No. rapportd is part of macOS: it lets your Apple devices nearby work together.

### Should I allow rapportd to accept incoming connections?

Yes, if you use Handoff, Universal Clipboard or other Continuity features between your devices. If you deny it, those features won't reach this Mac.

### Can I remove rapportd?

No. It's part of macOS. Quitting it only pauses it until macOS starts it again.
