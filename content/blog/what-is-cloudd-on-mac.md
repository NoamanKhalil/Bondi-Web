cloudd is the part of macOS that syncs your data with iCloud for Apple's apps and iCloud Drive. It's safe; when it's busy, it's handling a large sync, such as after signing in to iCloud or turning on a new iCloud feature.

## Key points

- cloudd syncs data with iCloud for Apple's apps and iCloud Drive.
- It gets busy with large syncs, such as after signing in or turning on a new iCloud feature.
- Stopping it doesn't help for long: macOS starts it again when it's needed.

## What cloudd does

Notes, Reminders, Photos, iCloud Drive and many other Apple apps keep your data in sync across your devices through cloudd. For iCloud Drive it works together with **bird**.

## Why cloudd is using CPU

Bondi's note: *Large syncs, such as after signing in or turning on a new iCloud feature.*

If it stays busy for a long time, look at what else is happening: a big download, an import, an update or a sync is usually the reason, and it settles once that finishes. If your whole Mac feels slow, [Why is my Mac slow?](/blog/why-is-my-mac-slow/) walks through the other usual causes.

## Can I quit cloudd?

Stopping it doesn't help for long: macOS starts it again when it's needed.

## Questions

### Is cloudd a virus?

No. cloudd is part of macOS: it syncs your data with iCloud for Apple's apps and iCloud Drive.

### Why is cloudd using CPU or network?

A large sync is running, for example right after signing in to iCloud or turning on a new iCloud feature. It settles when the sync is done.

### What happens if I quit cloudd?

Stopping it doesn't help for long: macOS starts it again when it's needed, and the sync carries on.
